The city

Nine crossroads in a grid. Cars enter from the edges and mostly drive straight. Every light takes turns: north–south green, then east–west green, once a minute.

The puzzle

For each crossroad we choose how long each direction gets green, and when its cycle starts. That’s 18 dials. Set them well and cars ride a “green wave” through several lights in a row.

Score = how long an average car waits at red lights. Lower is better.

The contestants

Five search methods copied from nature, plus a “traffic cop” that just reacts. Each searcher gets the same 3,000 tries in the simulator. Tap any box to see how it thinks and change its settings.

  • A light gives green to one direction at a time, in a 60-second loop. Every switch wastes 3 seconds of amber.
  • On green, about one car every 2 seconds gets through. Driving to the next crossroad takes 8 seconds.
  • Most cars go straight; a few turn left or right. Cars drive on the left, like in India.
  • Traffic builds up to a rush in the middle of a 15-minute window.
  • One “try” = simulating all 15 minutes with one set of light timings. A computer does this in under a millisecond.
Network
3×3 grid, 9 signalised junctions, 36 approaches. Traffic drives on the left.
Decision vector
18 numbers: a north–south green split and a cycle offset per junction.
Signals
60 s cycle, two phases, 3 s amber + all-red lost per phase.
Flow
Store-and-forward queues, 1800 veh/h saturation flow, 8 s link travel, 76% straight / 12% left / 12% right.
Demand
15-minute peak profile entering from the grid edges.
Objective
Average delay per vehicle (s). Vehicles still queued at the end are charged 90 s.
